# CleanMate features

Everything the Ubuntu app does, in detail. Install and overview: [README](../README.md).

## Desktop app


**Desktop app** — the first time it opens, a short welcome shows in three steps what CleanMate
cleans and how it keeps your files safe, what needs your password and what it never touches, and the
main options (language, automatic cleaning — user and developer junk and AI caches, only when plugged in
unless *Settings › Run even on battery* is on, retried within the hour when it fails, with its last run in
*Settings* —, scan reminder, low disk alert, sent by the app itself when it opens); in the Flatpak it shows
only what the Flatpak does (no password step, Uninstaller, terminal app or schedule) and links to its
limits, as *Settings* does; *main menu → Getting
Started…* shows it again; its last button, **Run Smart Scan**, starts the first scan. Then untick anything
you want to keep and press **Clean**. Click anywhere on a row to tick it; the **All** box on a group ticks or clears the whole
group. **Stop** ends a long scan and lists what it checked so far; the results say how long the scan took. **Ctrl+F** (or the search button) filters the results by name or path, including the parts of an item you opened. A folder listed inside an item (an old download, a stale build folder) has a ⋯ menu with **Never Clean This**: it goes to *Settings › Never clean* and scans skip it from then on (in app caches, app logs and temporary files the folder is kept while the rest is cleaned). **Why is this safe?** Every Smart Scan and Privacy item has an ⓘ button whose popover says what it is, where it is (its folders on this computer), what cleaning does (rebuilt by its app, moved to the Trash, or deleted for good, plus what you lose, such as being signed out of websites) and when it is skipped (files used recently, the newest version, while a program runs — a locked row names the program to close). **Open Folder** opens its folder; **Never Clean** puts the item's folders in *Settings › Never clean*, so scans leave the item out (Undo for 10 seconds). **Scan groups:** *Settings › Scan groups* turns a whole group (System Junk, Logs, User & App Junk, AI Tools, Developer Junk, Privacy) off: Smart Scan leaves it out in both apps (also `cm --scan`, `--clean` and automatic cleaning; `cm --only` still scans it), and the group's own page says it is off, with **Turn On**. A clean that ends while the window isn't in front sends a notification with what it freed (not in a dry run). Closing the window while something is still cleaning, moving to the Trash or putting back asks first: **Keep Running**, or **Stop and Close**, which ends the engine with what it started. CleanMate runs once: opening it again brings up the window already open. The app's text, notifications included (low disk, scan reminder, automatic cleaning), follows its language; the engine sends codes the app translates, while the terminal app stays in English. The app reopens on the page and at the size you left it. **Keyboard:** Ctrl+1…9 open System Overview, Smart Scan, the six cleanup pages and Space Lens, Ctrl+0 opens Large & Old Files and Ctrl+Shift+1…5 Duplicates, Uninstaller, Startup Items, Maintenance and Moved to the Trash, Ctrl+R (or F5) scans, searches or lists again on the open page, Ctrl+F searches what the open page lists (Smart Scan, Large & Old Files, Duplicates, Space Lens, Startup Items, Maintenance, Uninstaller, Moved to the Trash), and Ctrl+? lists every shortcut. On Large & Old Files, Duplicates and Space Lens a search hides the rows that don't match, the **All** box ticks only the rows listed and a ticked row the search hides is never moved; **Stop** ends a search or a measurement at once (Space Lens goes back to the folder shown before); a row's ⋯ menu has **Never Clean This Folder** (Duplicates: **Never Clean Its Folder**), which adds the folder to *Settings › Never clean* and takes what is in it off the list, with Undo. The confirmation lists every item with its size and where it goes — *To the Trash* or *Deleted permanently* — and the total of each. After a clean, a summary stays on top of the results until you scan again: space freed, apart from what went to the Trash (it only frees space once the Trash is emptied: **Empty Trash…** asks first, then empties it), *Free space: before → after* (for `/` and for your home folder when it is on another partition), how many items went to the Trash or were deleted permanently, **Open Trash**, **View Moved Items** (the Moved to the Trash page), and **Undo** for 10 seconds if the clean moved anything to the Trash (old installers and downloads, stale build folders, editor data of deleted projects) — everything else is deleted for good. Items with a 🔒 need your password (the standard Ubuntu dialog, which says what it's for: cleaning system files, uninstalling apps or Maintenance); only CleanMate installed from the PPA or the `.deb` asks for it. A copy in your home folder (`cm --install`, a git clone) asks for no password, so its 🔒 items, root apps in Uninstaller and root tasks in Maintenance are locked with *Needs CleanMate installed from a package* — clean them with `sudo cm` or install the package; everything else runs
as you. An item whose program is running is locked until you close it (found by its command line, program file or snap/Flatpak, so Gradle, Android Studio and other Java tools count too). An item whose check ran out of time is listed under
**Not finished** with **Scan Again**, and the header then says the scan didn't finish instead of calling the system clean. When
the engine fails, Smart Scan, Large & Old Files, Duplicates and Space Lens show what went wrong instead of an empty page —
the engine's last lines (your home folder hidden), **Try Again**, **Copy Details** and **Report a Bug…** — and items a crashed
clean never reached are marked failed with its reason. The sidebar shows the free space of `/` and of your home folder when
they are on different partitions; System Overview measures again after a clean. **Space Lens** lists what
takes up the space in your home folder (or any folder or drive), biggest first: click a folder to
dig deeper, tick what you don't need and move it to the Trash, **Undo** puts it back. On a writable
drive mounted under `/media`, `/run/media` or `/mnt` things go to that drive's own Trash, so the space
comes back on that drive; read-only and network drives, Timeshift backups and other places outside your
home folder it only shows. The Locations button opens your home folder, the whole system disk (`/`), each
other disk (with its free space) or any folder, and the path bar starts at the disk. Outside your home
folder every row has a lock whose tooltip says why it stays (system packages, Docker, the journal, logs,
snaps, another user's files, another disk mounted there) and an arrow to the page that cleans that kind of
thing (System Junk, Logs, Developer Junk, Uninstaller, Maintenance). A folder you can't read says *no
permission* instead of a size, and the header counts folders that couldn't be read, so `/` adds up to the
disk's use minus what only an administrator can see; CleanMate never reads as root here. **Ctrl+Shift+.**
(or the eye button) shows or hides names starting with a dot (one row sums up the hidden ones), and every
row has **Open** (its default app) and **Show in Files**. **Duplicates** groups identical files (from 1 MB, in a folder or drive you
pick); nothing is ticked until you choose, and **Select automatically** ticks every copy except the oldest
(usually the original); you're warned if every copy of a file is ticked. Under the title it says what it
left out: hidden files and folders, `node_modules`, Never clean folders, files it can't read or whose
names it can't show, and other drives mounted inside the folder. Its **Similar Photos** mode (desktop app only; the terminal app and `cm --json dupes` find identical files)
groups photos that look alike (resized or re-saved copies, edits, shots taken one after another) in
Pictures or a folder you pick, read on your computer only; it suggests keeping the one with the most
pixels, then the biggest file, ticks nothing until you choose, and moves the others to the Trash, with
Undo. JPEG and PNG always, WebP, HEIC and AVIF when your system can open them; hidden folders, Never
clean folders and Shotwell or digiKam libraries are skipped. Every photo row has a thumbnail, and the
photo you point at or move to with the keyboard shows large beside the list, the suggested one marked.
**Large & Old Files** lists
your biggest files (by minimum size, how long unused, and folder or drive); sort them by size, date
modified, name or kind, and show only one kind (videos, images, disk images and virtual machines,
archives, documents, other). "The N biggest of M" counts only files it can list; under the title it says
what it left out: files whose names have characters it can't show, files in caches, Git folders and Trash
folders, and other drives mounted inside the folder you picked, with **Search Other Drives Too**. What you move to the
Trash comes back with **Undo**, also from a USB or other drive (it goes to that drive's own Trash); elsewhere
outside your home folder it needs `gio` (`libglib2.0-bin`), and the error says so.
On all three pages images and videos show a thumbnail (the one your file manager made when there is one;
a video's frame needs GStreamer for Python, `gir1.2-gstreamer-1.0`, else it gets an icon), and **Open**
(or Enter on a row) opens the file in its default app before you decide; reading a file for its
thumbnail leaves its last-opened date alone. **Startup Items** switches the apps and your systemd user services
that start at login on or off (a service or timer also starts or stops right away; the system's own services are only shown); removing one of yours
moves its file to the Trash, with **Undo**; entries and services whose program is gone (left behind by an app you removed) are
listed first as broken, ready to remove. **Uninstaller** removes apt, Snap, Flatpak and AppImage apps — tick several (say,
everything unused for 12+ months) to remove them with one confirmation and one password; an app that is running
is skipped; an app that can't be removed keeps its row with the reason (a dismissed or missing password dialog says so) and the summary counts it, until **Back to the List** — and, if you tick them, the folders they leave in your home (to the Trash, with **Restore folders**); folders of apps you removed earlier are a group of their own
at the end, not ticked, with **Undo** after moving them to the Trash. **Reset…** keeps one app installed and moves
its data in your home to the Trash, so it starts as if new (pick which folders; **Restore** puts them back). **Moved to the Trash** (also *Settings › Moved to the Trash*) lists
everything CleanMate moved to the Trash — from any page, the terminal app or an earlier session — newest first, with
its original folder, the tool that moved it, the date and the size. Search by name or folder, show one tool only,
and **Put Back** one item, the ticked ones or all of them (**Put Back All…**): each returns to the folder it came
from, and one whose place another file has taken stays in the Trash. **Show in Files** shows it in the Trash.
Items emptied or deleted from the Trash in the last 30 days stay listed, greyed out, as *No longer in the Trash*.
**Maintenance** runs the terminal app's maintenance
tasks (updates, package repair, TRIM, font cache, locate…) with their output in a log window, asking for your
password once per task, says how much removing unused Flatpak runtimes frees, and switches the snap-versions and journal-size limits on or off; a task whose tool
isn't installed is locked. The app opens on **System Overview**: **Run Smart Scan** with when you last scanned, your system and package counts, each disk
with a usage bar (in the warning colour over 90 % full), a chart of free space over the last 30 days, memory
and swap, what CleanMate freed (the last 30 days, in total and the last cleanup, with a bar per day over the
last 30 days; space really freed, with "X in the Trash" under a number when part of it went to the Trash), space in use outside your folders (everything on the system disk outside your home folder,
with **View in Space Lens**; Timeshift snapshots, the swap file, ext4's reserved space), your largest folders, which open in Space Lens, and **System
protection**: whether the firewall (ufw), AppArmor, automatic security updates and Secure Boot are on. It
only reads them; one that is off shows the command that turns it on, with a button to copy it. One it
can't read shows *Unknown*.

## What it cleans


**Smart Scan** checks all of these at once (≈ 1 s):

| | What | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **System Junk** | Package and system leftovers | APT cache, PackageKit cache (packages and package lists GNOME Software and Software Updater downloaded; skipped while they run), unused packages, extra old kernels (a fallback always stays), leftover configs, old Snap revisions, unused Flatpak runtimes (runtimes and extensions no installed Flatpak app uses, in your own and the system installation, measured as `flatpak uninstall --unused` would remove them; skipped while Flatpak installs or updates; not in the Flatpak build), old temp files; old installers in Downloads (`.deb`, AppImage, `.iso`… over 30 days old by default — change it in Settings › Old installers; only a Downloads folder inside your home folder, not pre-selected, moved to the Trash; each file on its own in the desktop app); old downloads — files and folders at the top of Downloads you haven't opened or changed for 90 days (a folder counts as used when anything inside it was; change it in Settings › Old downloads; not pre-selected, moved to the Trash; each one on its own in the desktop app); old screenshots — screenshots and screen recordings (`Screenshot from …`, `Screencast from …`, `Screenshot_<date>…`) at the top of Pictures or Pictures/Screenshots untouched for as long (same setting; not pre-selected, moved to the Trash; each file on its own in the desktop app); abandoned downloads — unfinished `.crdownload`, `.part`, `.aria2` and `.!qB` files at the top of Downloads untouched for 7 days, together with the file of the real name Firefox and aria2 keep next to them (not pre-selected, moved to the Trash; each download on its own in the desktop app) |
| **Logs** | Logs nobody reads | systemd journal (keeps 7 days), rotated logs, oversized live logs (e.g. a 300 MB `syslog`), crash reports, core dumps, old app logs |
| **User & App Junk** | Your caches | Trash, the Trash on USB and other drives (not pre-selected; each drive on its own in the desktop app), thumbnails, stale caches, GPU shader caches (not pre-selected), game launcher caches (winetricks and Wine Gecko/Mono installers, Lutris and Heroic cover art and temporary installers, deb and Flatpak; one row per folder, skipped while a launcher, Wine or Proton runs; Wine prefixes and saves are never touched), Steam caches (the web cache of its store and library pages, depot manifests, logs and crash dumps, for deb, Flatpak and Snap Steam; one row per folder, skipped while Steam runs; games, downloads, Proton prefixes, saves and your sign-in are never touched), Minecraft caches (the official launcher's and Prism Launcher's logs, crash reports and web or download cache, deb and Flatpak; one row per folder, skipped while a launcher or the game runs — other Java programs such as IDEs or Gradle don't block it; worlds, mods, resource packs, screenshots, settings and accounts are never touched), the desktop search indexes of GNOME (Tracker / LocalSearch) and KDE (Baloo) — not pre-selected, reset with the indexer's own command when installed, settings kept — and **each browser** and **each app** (VS Code, Slack, Discord, Steam's shader cache, Dropbox, Nextcloud, ownCloud and MEGAsync caches, the Telegram Desktop media cache and the Spotify cache (not pre-selected; Spotify's cache also holds songs downloaded for offline listening), Snap and Flatpak apps) as its own choice |
| **AI Tools** | AI assistant clutter | caches, logs and *old* sessions of Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Codex, Gemini/Antigravity, Cursor, Windsurf, opencode, Cline, Kilo Code, MiMoCode, ZCode, Zed, Copilot CLI, Continue; data of deleted projects in VS Code, Cursor, Windsurf and VSCodium (`workspaceStorage`, also the Snap and Flatpak builds; not pre-selected, moved to the Trash, skipped while the editor runs; each project on its own in the desktop app) |
| **AI Models** | Downloaded models | **each** Ollama, Hugging Face and LM Studio model on its own — never pre-selected, never cleaned automatically; interrupted Ollama downloads (`sha256-…-partial`, also in `OLLAMA_MODELS`) are one more item, skipped while Ollama runs (LM Studio: `~/.lmstudio/models`, `~/.cache/lm-studio/models` and the folder set in its My Models); models PyTorch Hub, Whisper, sentence-transformers and Keras downloaded (`~/.cache/torch`, `~/.cache/whisper`, `~/.keras`, also `TORCH_HOME`, `SENTENCE_TRANSFORMERS_HOME`, `KERAS_HOME`, `XDG_CACHE_HOME`) are one more item, each model on its own in the desktop app; a model a program has open is skipped |
| **Developer Junk** | Build and package caches | npm, Yarn, pnpm, Bun, pip, uv, Go, Cargo, rustup downloads, Gradle, Maven, Conda, Deno, Zig, Bazel, ccache, sccache, NuGet, Poetry, pre-commit, pipx run; infrastructure tools — the Terraform/OpenTofu plugin cache (`~/.terraform.d/plugin-cache`, `TF_PLUGIN_CACHE_DIR`, `plugin_cache_dir` in `~/.terraformrc`) and the minikube download cache (`~/.minikube/cache`: ISO, preloaded images, binaries; clusters, profiles and certificates are kept), both not pre-selected and deleted, and the Helm cache (`~/.cache/helm`; repositories and plugins are kept); language package caches — Haskell (cabal `~/.cabal/packages` and `~/.cache/cabal/packages`, Stack `~/.stack/pantry` and `snapshots`, ghcup `~/.ghcup/cache`; installed GHC versions and tools are kept), Scala (Coursier `~/.cache/coursier`, `~/.ivy2/cache`, `~/.sbt/boot`; `~/.ivy2/local` is kept) and OCaml (`~/.opam/download-cache`), not pre-selected, and Composer (`~/.cache/composer`, `~/.composer/cache`); `CABAL_DIR`, `STACK_ROOT`, `COURSIER_CACHE`, `OPAMROOT`, `COMPOSER_HOME` and `COMPOSER_CACHE_DIR` are followed inside your home; each skipped while its tool runs; Docker images, build cache, containers and volumes (each separately; in the desktop app also one by one: dangling images, build-cache records, unused images and unused volumes — a named volume is marked *may hold data*; with Docker 23 or newer, cleaning the whole volumes item removes only anonymous volumes, so its size counts only those); stale `node_modules` and build folders count only what deleting frees (files pnpm or Bun hard-linked from their store are left out); Podman; Playwright, Puppeteer and Cypress browsers (never pre-selected); Firebase CLI logs and downloaded emulators (not pre-selected); the Dart and Flutter pub cache (hosted packages and git cache, not pre-selected, skipped while dart or flutter runs); old runtime versions — every version nvm (Node.js), SDKMAN, fvm (Flutter), pyenv (Python), rbenv (Ruby) or rustup (Rust toolchains) installed, and every Go SDK in `~/sdk`, that isn't your default (nvm's `alias/default` and other aliases, the `node` on your PATH, SDKMAN's `current`, fvm's `default`, what pyenv's and rbenv's `version` file names, rustup's default toolchain and overrides, the Go SDK your `go` runs from are kept, as is a pyenv version holding virtualenvs; a tool without a default keeps every version; not pre-selected, deleted, a version a program runs from is skipped; each version on its own in the desktop app); old asdf and mise runtime versions — every version asdf or mise installed that is neither the newest of its tool nor pinned in `~/.tool-versions`, mise's settings or the `.tool-versions` / `mise.toml` of a project in your home (a pin like `20` or `lts` keeps the newest match; mise's alias links are never touched; not pre-selected, deleted, skipped while asdf or mise runs, a version a program runs from is skipped; each version on its own in the desktop app); old Vagrant box versions (`~/.vagrant.d/boxes` or `VAGRANT_HOME`, 0.5–2 GB each) — the newest version of each box and every version a machine uses (Vagrant's machine index) are kept, and nothing is offered when the index can't be read; only files are read, projects' `.vagrant` folders and libvirt volumes are never touched (not pre-selected, deleted, skipped while vagrant runs; each version on its own in the desktop app); old Unity Editor versions in Unity Hub (`~/Unity/Hub/Editor/<version>` and Unity Hub's install locations, often 5–15 GB each) — the newest and every version a project uses (`ProjectSettings/ProjectVersion.txt` of Unity Hub's projects and of projects in your home) are kept (not pre-selected, deleted, skipped while Unity or Unity Hub runs, a version a program runs from is skipped; each version on its own in the desktop app); Unity caches (`~/.cache/unity3d`, `~/.config/unity3d/cache`, Asset Store packages in `~/.local/share/unity3d`; not pre-selected, skipped while Unity runs; licenses, settings and game saves are kept); `node_modules` of projects idle for 90 days; build folders of projects idle for 90 days, each only next to the project file that makes it — Cargo and Maven `target/`, Gradle/Android `build/`, `.gradle/` and module `build/` folders (`app/build/`), Flutter `.dart_tool/` and `build/`, SwiftPM `.build/`, Next.js `.next/`, Nuxt `.nuxt/` and `.output/`, SvelteKit `.svelte-kit/`, Angular `.angular/cache/`, Turborepo `.turbo/`, Haskell `.stack-work/` and `dist-newstyle/`, Elixir `_build/` and `deps/`, Zig `.zig-cache/` (never `zig-out/`), Python `.venv/`, CocoaPods `Pods/`, Terraform/OpenTofu `.terraform/providers` and `.terraform/modules` (never state files) (not pre-selected, moved to the Trash; each project on its own in the desktop app); Android emulator system images no virtual device uses (`~/Android/Sdk`, `ANDROID_HOME`, `ANDROID_SDK_ROOT`; not pre-selected, deleted, skipped while Android Studio, the emulator or sdkmanager runs; each image on its own in the desktop app); Android emulator snapshots — the `snapshots` folder of each virtual device (AVD), often GBs each; the next start is a cold boot, the device's apps and data stay (not pre-selected, deleted, skipped while the emulator runs; each AVD on its own in the desktop app); old Android SDK components — NDK, build-tools, SDK platform and CMake versions an SDK Manager update leaves behind; the newest of each kind and the versions a project in your home pins (`ndkVersion`, `buildToolsVersion`, `compileSdk` in `build.gradle(.kts)`, `ndk.dir` in `local.properties`) are kept, `platform-tools`, `emulator` and system images are never touched (not pre-selected, deleted, skipped while Android Studio, Gradle or the emulator runs; each version on its own in the desktop app); old Proton and Wine versions (Proton-GE, Wine-GE, often 0.5–1 GB each) in Steam's `compatibilitytools.d`, Lutris, Heroic and Bottles, deb and Flatpak — the newest of each and the versions a game or bottle is set to use are kept, Steam's own Proton is never touched (not pre-selected, deleted, skipped while a launcher, Wine, Proton or gamescope runs; each version on its own in the desktop app); old JetBrains IDE and Android Studio versions — the settings, plugins, caches and logs an upgrade leaves behind (`IntelliJIdea2023.1`, `AndroidStudio2023.3`… in `~/.config`, `~/.local/share` and `~/.cache`, also Flatpak and Snap builds) when a newer version of the same IDE is there; never the newest, not pre-selected, moved to the Trash, skipped while that IDE runs; each version on its own in the desktop app; old extension versions of VS Code, VS Code Insiders, VSCodium, Cursor and Windsurf (`~/.vscode/extensions` and the like, also the Flatpak builds) — the folders an extension update leaves behind, and versions the editor marked obsolete; the newest version and any version the editor's `extensions.json` names are kept (not pre-selected, deleted, skipped while that editor runs; each extension on its own in the desktop app) |

**Privacy** (never part of Smart Scan) clears shell history (bash, zsh, fish), *Recent files*,
Python/Node/database shell history, Vim history, and the Alt+F2 and emoji history. Each browser
(Firefox, Chrome, Chromium, Brave, Edge, Vivaldi, Opera; deb, Snap and Flatpak, every profile) is
its own item: browsing history, the downloads list and cookies, which signs you out of websites.
Passwords, bookmarks, extensions, saved cards and settings are kept. Not pre-selected, skipped while
the browser runs; Firefox needs `sqlite3` (it clears the records, so bookmarks in the same file stay).

**More tools in the terminal app** (Space Lens, Large & Old Files, Duplicates, the Uninstaller, Startup Items, Maintenance, System Overview and Settings are in the desktop app too):

- **Uninstaller** — every app you can open (apt, Snap, Flatpak, and AppImage apps with a launcher in
  `~/.local/share/applications`), with its real size (the app *plus* its folders in your home) and when
  you last used it. An AppImage app goes to the Trash as you — the file, its launcher and the icon only
  it uses — so you can put it back. Sort by size or last use, or show only apps
  you haven't opened in 90+ or 365+ days (**f** in the terminal app; desktop app: unused for 1, 3, 6 or 12+ months). Parts of Ubuntu itself are hidden, and a removal that would take
  something Ubuntu needs with it (GNOME Shell, the login screen, sudo, the running kernel…) is
  refused. Afterwards it offers the app's own folders for the Trash — never folders other apps share.
  The last row, **Leftovers of removed apps**, lists folders no installed app uses any more:
  `~/.var/app/<id>` of a Flatpak app and `~/snap/<name>` of a snap that are gone, and folders in
  `~/.config`, `~/.local/share`, `~/.local/state` and `~/.cache` whose name matches no package, snap,
  Flatpak app, launcher, command (also from the PATH your shell profile sets) or running program.
  Folders the desktop or many apps share (dconf, GTK, fonts, keyrings…), folders with programs in
  them, changed in the last 30 days or with a file open are never listed. Nothing is ticked; they go
  to the Trash (`z` puts them back).
  `r` resets the selected app: it stays installed, its folders in your home (for a Flatpak app its
  cache, config and data; for a snap its current revision and `common`) go to the Trash, so it
  starts as if new — `z` puts them back. Refused while the app runs.
- **Maintenance** — update everything (with a list of what will change first), repair packages,
  TRIM, free RAM, and lasting limits so old snap versions and system logs don't pile up again.
- **System Overview** — storage, free-space history, space outside your folders (Timeshift
  snapshots, the swap file, ext4's reserved space), your largest folders, and whether the firewall,
  AppArmor, automatic security updates and Secure Boot are on (read only, with the command to fix one).
- Space Lens (browse folders by size), Large & Old Files (the same files and order as the desktop
  app, with what it left out), Duplicates (also with what it left out), Startup Items (login apps;
  **t** moves a broken one to the Trash) and Settings.
